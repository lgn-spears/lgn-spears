# Logan Spears

I build the systems that sit under support operations. Intake, routing, triage,
escalation, and the audit trail that tells you why something went sideways.

### Where that came from.

Nine years at Apple Retail, most of it as the person between the store and the
engineers who built what we used. I ran our ticket pipeline and ended up the
number one ticket creator in Apple Retail, then spent two years on a weekly call
with the engineering support org tracing problems back to what was actually
causing them. Twice I covered an operations lead role, once for the first
online-only store opening Apple ever did.

The thing that stuck: the reported problem is usually not the real one, and the
fix usually belongs to a system further upstream than the person reporting it.

### What's here.

**harness-cl** — gated self-improvement for AI agents, plus an adversarial
analysis of the safety gates themselves.

**cognitive-core** — a clock for AI agents. Elapsed-time awareness, session
journals, advice dedup. Zero dependencies, one script.

### What isn't.

Most of what I build is private.

**Canopy** — landscape management software. The industry runs on 1–3% profit, and
a lot of that is owners working off old tools, cut off from their own financial
cycle. Canopy connects the parts that normally never talk to each other, from
what you charge for a job to what it actually cost you, and teaches the owner how
to run the company while they use it. Same instinct as the Apple work: fix the
tool.

Alongside that: a lead intake and routing system running today for a paying
customer, financial analysis tooling for a CPA-led consultancy, and a complaints
triage pipeline built on public regulatory data.

### Open to work.

Support and operations systems, escalations, anything where the queue is the
signal and somebody has to work out why it keeps filling up.
